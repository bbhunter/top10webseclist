# All historical years: missed-reference audit — 10 September 2026

**41 newly judged techniques were added across 2006–2025.** Each passed the full six-category review at 55 or above with a qualifying non-duplicate verdict. No new addition qualified for 2012. Original winners and nomination sections were preserved; additions are in each year’s existing “Missed from the original list” section. 2016 and 2017 were searched independently and share `2016-17.md`.

This repeats the historical discovery workflow and follows selected research-bearing references from the archived Markdown, as requested. It is a bounded search of every year, not a claim that every internet source or every outbound link has been read. The dated year ledgers retain source URLs, queries, rejected prescreens, unresolved leads, prior-art comparisons and unswept beats. Draft recommendations in those ledgers are historical handoffs; final scorecards and the table below control the additions.

## Citation tracing and disclosure corrections

The citation inventory covered **1,592 archived Markdown files** in the historical collections, including the shared 2016–17 collection once. Its URL counts include non-research material and were used only to select promising references for reading. Inventory normalization excluded URLs already represented in curated lists and the manifest; mechanism checks then caught duplicates whose titles or URLs differed.

Examples where tracing helped:

- The 2014 malicious-IdP paper led back to Arnott’s 2009 association-poisoning disclosure, which received its own historical judgement.
- The LinkedIn archive contains a May 2015 Netty cookie article distinct from the nominated November research despite a shared page URL.
- The browser whitepaper’s citations led to Heyes’s 2016 JSON-hijacking extension.
- DOM-XSS studies led to earlier exploit-generation and sanitizer-analysis methods. The recovered 2012/2014 automation prior narrowed the 2022 Hand Sanitizers score to 72.7.
- Shared Storage’s 2025 paper led to substantive public issues in 2022, 2023 and 2024. Those dated increments were evaluated in their original years; a distinct 2025 paper contribution remains held.
- Sage’s local-file feed behavior was found in the already-nominated Hacking RSS Feeds material, resolving that apparent miss as represented.

Live browser exploits and supplied PoCs were not executed. A failed backward search is never treated as proof of priority.
