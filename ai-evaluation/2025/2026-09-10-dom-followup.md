## Core contribution and evidence digest

The transferable rule is to treat explicit DOM selection and property reads as an input trust boundary: benign-looking markup can supply data consumed by existing code in requests, links, resource loads and other sensitive operations. This paper measures that boundary beyond script execution, extends Foxhound's DOM-source tracking and JAW's graph queries, and looks for markup injection on pages where gadgets occur. The strongest defensible contribution is this integrated analysis and measurement, with limited additional selector-order adaptations; it is not the invention of benign-markup code reuse, request hijacking, automated gadget verification or all four claimed parser techniques.

The study records approximately 2.64 million DOM-to-sink flows across 522,860 pages from 15,000 seed sites. Dynamic gadget verification inserts generated markup into a response before parsing and checks whether the entire marker reaches its sink. It verifies 357,982 gadget flows on 2,259 sites. The pipeline separately validates markup-injection flows from web-attacker inputs and reports 657 gadget/injection pairs on 177 pages across 37 sites. Static analysis adds coverage unavailable at page load and permits examination of validation patterns. These are useful methodological/data contributions even when underlying sinks are known.

Important evidence boundaries from §§5.3–5.5 and 6.5–6.9:

1. A verified marker flow establishes influence, not necessarily a harmful action. The paper itself gives a harmless language-query-parameter example. Its manual phase uses response rewriting to simulate the initial markup injection and code inspection to assess request impact.
2. Of 4,722 verified injection flows, 4,379 already support direct XSS and 343 do not. Do not equate every one of the 657 paired flows with a newly demonstrated CSP/sanitizer bypass; the paper does not provide that complete per-pair impact decomposition.
3. The two detailed case studies expressly lack a currently available markup injection. They illustrate conditional gadget exploitation. The request gadget retrieves attacker-selected response content and inserts it as HTML; the markup gadget is acknowledged to resemble the 2017 script-gadget class. They do not prove 657 independently completed live attacks.
4. Manual review of 440 static flows found 126 false positives (28.6%). Some gadgets merely read/write the same element, and implicit influence is not necessarily attacker control. Pattern absence is not proof that every flow has exploitable, unchecked input.
5. The reported 34% ordering figure is a comparison of 253,000 gadget/injection flow combinations. In 34% the injection occurs after the selected element; 57% cannot be compared. This measures an ordering obstacle. It does **not** demonstrate that the proposed techniques resolve all those cases, or that all require a new 2025 method; two methods already appeared in 2022.
6. Table 5 has zero dynamically verified WebSocket or navigation gadgets despite potential flows in those classes. Table 8's paired cases cover markup, request URL/body, iframe source and links. Avoid treating the entire taxonomy as equally demonstrated production exploitability.
7. The benchmark detects 8/13 known script-gadget library cases (38.5% false negatives); coverage excludes authentication and most interaction, and static analysis covers ten pages/site. These limitations temper practical claims without invalidating the narrower evidence.

### Practical constraints on the remaining ordering contribution

Old parser rules support the mechanisms but also narrow them. Attribute merging does not overwrite an already present root attribute. Fragment parsing and template contexts can ignore html/body/frameset tokens. A frameset replacement requires the parser's `frameset-ok` state; merely omitting a literal body tag does not guarantee success after arbitrary content. Foster parenting requires an appropriate table parsing context; content inside a normal table cell is not automatically moved outside the table. Candidate Table 7 also shows html/body blocked by default in the examined sanitizers, with special DOMPurify configurations changing that result. These observations qualify broad applicability, rather than refuting the demonstrated narrower primitives.

**Exact marginal delta:** a DOM-source-focused extension and empirical combination of established gadget discovery, established request-hijacking sinks and separately verified injection discovery; a selector-complexity/validation study; and two potentially distinct parser-to-selector adaptations (table and frameset). Two of the four proposed ordering techniques have direct 2022 attack precedent. No evidence supports branding the whole result a newly invented DOM-gadget attack class or the first automated gadget detector.
