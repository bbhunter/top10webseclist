## Survivor: PDFex — Practical Decryption exFiltration: Breaking PDF Encryption

**Primary source:** [15-page CCS paper](https://www.pdf-insecurity.org/download/paper-pdf_encryption-ccs2019.pdf), Jens Müller, Fabian Ising, Vladislav Mladenov, Christian Mainka, Sebastian Schinzel and Jörg Schwenk. [Author announcement](https://web-in-security.blogspot.com/2019/09/pdfex-major-security-flaws-in-pdf.html) explicitly dates publication **30 September 2019**, independently corroborated by the [research group's announcement](https://www.nds.rub.de/chair/news/articles/ccs19/index.html). November CCS presentation is not the cutoff. No earlier public disclosure was verified in this sweep.

**Read evidence:** full paper including sections 1–9, references and appendix; local `/tmp/websec-sweep-2019/pdfex.pdf` and `pdfex.txt` (15 pages, 87,530 extracted characters). Tables 1 and 2 were also rendered and visually inspected in `pdfex-p9.png` and `pdfex-p11.png`, because extraction loses circle glyphs. The technical [project page](https://www.pdf-insecurity.org/encryption/encryption.html) was read too. Published findings are evidence, not independently reproduced experiments.

**Evidence digest:** An attacker who can modify ciphertext and deliver it to its legitimate recipient can exploit PDF object references to move decrypted secrets into forms, links or script requests. Partial encryption enables one route; another repurposes the AESV3 `Perms` field as known plaintext for CBC gadgets. PDF-specific compression and object-stream handling extend the construction. Browser viewers were tested alongside desktop products. The paper reports some leakage in all 27 viewers, but full plaintext in only 21; other results are limited to uncompressed content or single words. User interaction varies. These distinctions matter more than an unqualified “breaks every PDF” claim. The web connection is direct: browser PDF viewers and HTTP-capable PDF actions cross the confidentiality boundary.

**Closest pre-cutoff comparison, rechecked:** [EFAIL, USENIX 2018](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-poddebniak.pdf), especially §§3–5, already demonstrates CBC/CFB exfiltration gadgets, chosen blocks, ignored garbage and **Deflate backreferences that remove random fragments**. Thus neither CBC gadgets nor compression-assisted exfiltration is new in 2019. PDFex contributes the PDF object/crypt-filter combination, document-wide AESV3 permission-block gadget source, and PDF-specific object-stream/half-open-string engineering. This is a **meaningful combination/adaptation**, not a new cryptographic attack class. Older PDF actions and malicious URI handling are further predecessors identified in its bibliography; those alone do not establish encrypted-document plaintext recovery.

**Fresh skeptical pass:** In addition to the paper's EFAIL citation, alternate searches explicitly sought encrypted-PDF `Identity` abuse and pre-September-2019 exfiltration. Results supplied brute-force PDF password work, prior PDF malware and later repetitions; none established this exact encrypted-object construction before the cutoff. Absence of a match is not proof of priority. Local year/archive filename and text searches for PDFex, EFAIL, malicious URI/origami and PDF encryption found no existing 2019 nomination of this technique; the 2020 PDF-injection calibration already references PDFex as prior art, which is a discovery corroboration only and contributes no hindsight to the scores.
