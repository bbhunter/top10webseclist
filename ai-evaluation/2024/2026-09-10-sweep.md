## Survivor: Shared Storage worklet queue and termination channels, February 2024 disclosures

**Primary sources and date:** Alex Nisenoff's [Shared Storage return-time report, issue 136](https://github.com/WICG/shared-storage/issues/136), opened **20 February 2024 01:11:54 UTC**; [2 February 2024 crash-channel comment](https://github.com/WICG/shared-storage/issues/86#issuecomment-1924746302), with [20 February embedder-crash observation](https://github.com/WICG/shared-storage/issues/86#issuecomment-1954416877) and [21 February maintainer confirmation](https://github.com/WICG/shared-storage/issues/86#issuecomment-1956795524). Complete issue bodies and comment histories were read via the public API; copies `/tmp/websec-sweep-2024/shared-136.json` and `shared-86.json`. All three original issue-136 histograms were fetched and visually inspected, `/tmp/websec-sweep-2024/queue-0.png` through `queue-2.png`. Both issue URLs pass the year exclusion filter. The two channels may be grouped in a single 2024 entry with separate links; do not assign the entire later 2025 paper to 2024.

**Contribution stripped of platform detail:** operations inside a restricted reader can alter completion timing or process survival visible to a less-privileged context. An explicit output budget does not cover these alternate observations. This is an adaptation of established covert channels to a specific new privacy boundary, not invention of timing or termination channels.

**Full-source digest:** the queue report compares external `sharedStorage.set` return times with an idle worklet, a constantly writing worklet, and a worklet alternating two seconds of writes and inactivity. Chrome 121 produces clearly different return distributions; the periodic case exhibits large bursts. The reporter explains that internal activity can depend on a stored secret, allowing external code to infer worklet activity. The 2024 report establishes this observable coupling; the exact database scheduler explanation and later channel-capacity results must not be imported from the 2025 paper. The February 27 maintainer response acknowledges it and discusses instant returns, rate limits and worklet-duration limits.

The February 2 comment describes conditionally crashing a worklet before `selectURL` returns. Presence or absence of a network request then communicates a bit without needing a conspicuous timing interval. The reporter says a single-URL call incurs no selection-budget charge; the maintainer agrees with the underlying leak on February 8. February 20–21 adds a distinct local observation: the caller's same-origin page crashes too because worklet and embedder share a process. That can escape mitigations limited to the selected frame's network traffic. Array logging or resource-intensive array computation is given as a crash trigger. No general exploitation rate, reliability benchmark or complete encoder/decoder is supplied in these 2024 comments.

**Closest prior art and skeptical comparison:** [issue 86's original 5 June 2023 report](https://github.com/WICG/shared-storage/issues/86) already shows budget-free identifier leakage by delaying the worklet result and observing network requests. That original delayed-network method belongs in 2023. The narrower 2024 additions are observable storage-operation contention and conditional termination/shared-process crash propagation. [Loophole: Timing Attacks on Shared Event Loops in Chrome, 2017](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity17/technical-sessions/presentation/vila), local `archived-references/md/2016-17/` Loophole paper, already monitors event-loop work by queuing short tasks and timing their dispatch; its host-process channel even communicates across separate/incognito tabs. Its primary abstract and §3.2 were reread. Thus the queue principle is old, while the experimentally identified Shared Storage reader-to-writer boundary and relationship to its output budget supply the specific new capability. Earlier general termination/storage channels are background; no claim of first termination channel is warranted.

Fresh backward searches: `"shared storage" "timing" before:2024-02-20`; `"sharedStorage" "set" "side channel" before:2024-02-20`; `"sharedStorage" "queue" before:2024-02-20`; `"sharedStorage" "side-channel" before:2024-02-01`; `"worklet" "crash" "shared storage" before:2024-02-02`; `"Shared Storage" "termination" before:2024-02-02`; `"Shared Storage" "covert channel" before:2024-02-20`; `browser covert channel "event loop" 2017`. Most generic “shared storage” results concern unrelated operating-system storage. The precise searches returned the later paper and no independently verified earlier identical API channels. That negative search does not prove absolute priority. Later 2025 measurements were excluded from the historical draft scores.

**Draft verdict: Meaningful combination/adaptation, medium confidence.** This tests a browser privacy mechanism and therefore has a direct web-security connection. It requires attacker-controlled worklet and observing page, availability of Shared Storage, and the reported browser implementation. It is not a claim that arbitrary unrelated secrets are readable.

### 2. The Great Request Robbery / Sheriff — 2023 release, 2024 paper

[Paper](https://trouge.net/papers/sp24_request_hijacking.pdf), all 19 pages read including appendices and references; `/tmp/websec-sweep-2024/robbery.pdf` and `.txt`. [IEEE program](https://sp2024.ieee-security.org/accepted-papers.html) confirms May 20–23, 2024. June/July 2023 in the paper describe private vulnerability notification, which alone would not date public research.

However, primary repository history establishes substantive earlier tooling: [28 October 2023 release commit](https://github.com/SoheilKhodayari/JAW/commit/c74fcfe6b62a6d93901f65ffd4d7483c2aab2b89) and [its graph builder](https://github.com/SoheilKhodayari/JAW/blob/c74fcfe6b62a6d93901f65ffd4d7483c2aab2b89/engine/lib/jaw/graphbuilder.js) contain `mapFoxhoundTaintFlowsToGraph`. The [20 December 2023 README](https://github.com/SoheilKhodayari/JAW/blob/a3dcc93edc8af1c14a74d255dddebf3db25b54e5/README.md) explicitly identifies October's JAW-v3/Sheriff release. Historical analysis source calls dynamic-Foxhound-edge construction, and semantic types cover WebSockets and EventSource. Copies: `jaw-oct2023-graphbuilder.js`, `jaw-2023-readme.md`, `jaw-2023-static_analysis.js`, `jaw-2023-semantic_types.js`, and `jaw-graphbuilder-history.json` in the same temporary directory. Source files were read, not executed.

The earlier index's “same mechanism as CSPT2CSRF” screening is too broad to explain all of this contribution: the paper augments JAW's hybrid property graph with dynamic call/data edges, extends sink instrumentation, and verifies source-controlled request parameters. [JAW 2021](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity21/presentation/khodayari) already uses graph traversals, dynamic environment state and event traces; its archived §3.2 was compared. Sheriff's finer dynamic-taint graph repair and broader API coverage are concrete methodology extensions. The 2024 paper reports 17.9K mixed paths and 67 manually constructed exploits, but those later results cannot establish what was public or support publication-time scoring in October 2023. Reported 202K “verified” flows are parameter influence, not 202K confirmed harmful exploits. Held for 2024: no separately dated 2024 technical delta established. Primary 2023 lead transferred to that year's reviewer.

### 3. PanoptiChrome — 2023 patch, 2024 paper

[Author paper](https://srsarangi.github.io/files/papers/panoptichrome.pdf), [ACM record](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589334.3645699): 13 May 2024. All nine pages read; working files `panoptichrome.pdf` and `.txt`. It describes modern Chromium object-taint tracking with runtime sources/sinks, restricted interpreter instrumentation, ephemeron weak references and persistent handles to accommodate garbage collection. It inherits hybrid static/runtime taint propagation from [Mystique 2018](https://kapravelos.com/papers/2018/07/30/mystique-ccs/), not a new taint primitive. The publication's fingerprinting totals are potential/analyst-classified APIs, not demonstrated independent identifying entropy; the introduction's 164 and later 208 counts differ, and no end-to-end runtime overhead evaluation is supplied.

Date problem: the public repository was created **12 October 2023**, and its main patch's only listed commit is [d0725658093811a5ed67dd73bc20f946443c0c11](https://github.com/therahulkanyal/PanoptiChrome/commit/d0725658093811a5ed67dd73bc20f946443c0c11), 12 October 2023 12:10:04 UTC. The [historical 233,878-character patch](https://github.com/therahulkanyal/PanoptiChrome/blob/d0725658093811a5ed67dd73bc20f946443c0c11/panoptichrome_patches.diff) contains actual taint machinery, ephemeron tables and persistent handles. Saved `panoptic-patch-history.json` and `panoptic-2023-panoptichrome_patches.diff`. Commit time plus repository creation is strong early-release evidence, though precise initial public visibility was not independently reconstructed. Held for 2024 rather than treating the proceedings date as first disclosure; transferred to 2023 reviewer. No 2024 final/draft judgement advanced after this date evidence appeared.
