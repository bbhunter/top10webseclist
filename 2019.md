## Missed from the original list

> **Retrospective research additions.** These references were first published in
> 2019, were not present in the original nomination set above, and cleared the
> repository's 60 or above judgement gate as qualifying non-duplicates. Scores
> and prior-art decisions are preserved in [`ai-evaluation/2019/`](ai-evaluation/2019/).

-   [Turbo Intruder: Embracing the billion-request attack](https://portswigger.net/research/turbo-intruder-embracing-the-billion-request-attack) — James Kettle — A purpose-built HTTP stack, programmable request engine, connection gating and differential analysis make huge-volume and timing-sensitive web attacks practical. **Score: 89.2/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [Cross-Origin State Inference (COSI) Attacks: Leaking Web Site States through XS-Leaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02204) — Avinash Sudhodanan, Soheil Khodayari, Juan Caballero — Formalises multi-state cross-origin inference, composes leak classes across browsers and adds the Basta-COSI attack generator. **Score: 88.9/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [Iframes/Popups Are Dangerous in Mobile WebView: Studying and Mitigating Differential Context Vulnerabilities](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/yang-guangliang) — GuangLiang Yang, Jeff Huang, Guofei Gu — Defines attacks caused by browser/WebView context differences, including hidden origins, bridge-validation bypasses and trusted-UI overlap. **Score: 87.3/100 — Original technique.**
-   [Leaky Images: Targeted Privacy Attacks in the Web](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/staicu) — Cristian-Alexandru Staicu, Michael Pradel — Turns privately shared attacker-controlled images into cross-origin presence and identity-linking oracles for chosen users. **Score: 86.5/100 — Meaningful combination or adaptation.**
-   [Bypassing CSP with policy injection](https://portswigger.net/research/bypassing-csp-with-policy-injection) — Gareth Heyes — Shows that injected CSP directives and browser parsing differences can discard or override intended script restrictions. **Score: 83.8/100 — Meaningful extension.**
-   [JavaScript Template Attacks: Automatically Inferring Host Information for Targeted Exploits](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ndss2019_01B-4_Schwarz_paper.pdf) — Michael Schwarz, Florian Lackner, Daniel Gruss — Automatically learns host-dependent JavaScript behaviour to infer exploit-relevant properties such as CPU architecture and allocator behaviour. **Score: 83.5/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [What Are You Searching For? A Remote Keylogging Attack on Search Engine Autocomplete](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/monaco) — John V. Monaco — Combines timing, encoded-space length changes and HTTP/2 Huffman lengths to infer queries from TLS-encrypted autocomplete traffic. **Score: 83.2/100 — Meaningful combination or adaptation.**
-   [Cross-Site Challenge-Response Attacks](https://madweb.work/papers/2019/paper4.pdf) — Nethanel Gelernter, Itamar Peretz — Uses cross-origin response differentiation and visitors' browsers to brute-force challenge-response values incorrectly used as CSRF protection. **Score: 82.6/100 — Meaningful combination or adaptation.**
-   [PhishFarm: A Scalable Framework for Measuring the Effectiveness of Evasion Techniques against Browser Phishing Blacklists](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2019.00049) — Adam Oest, Yeganeh Safaei, Adam Doupé, Gail-Joon Ahn, Brad Wardman, Kevin Tyers — Safely deploys controlled phishing sites to measure how individual HTTP cloaking filters evade production browser blacklists. **Score: 81.5/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [All Your Clicks Belong to Me: Investigating Click Interception on the Web](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/zhang) — Mingxue Zhang, Wei Meng, Sangho Lee, Byoungyoung Lee, Xinyu Xing — Instruments browser event registration and dispatch to detect, classify and attribute third-party JavaScript click interception. **Score: 80.7/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [Latex Gloves: Protecting Browser Extensions from Probing and Revelation Attacks](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ndss2019_01B-5_Sjosten_paper.pdf) — Alexander Sjösten, Steven Van Acker, Pablo Picazo-Sanchez, Andrei Sabelfeld — Extends extension fingerprinting with DOM revelation channels and shows that random extension identifiers can strengthen per-user uniqueness. **Score: 79.9/100 — Meaningful extension.**
-   [Scalable Scanning and Automatic Classification of TLS Padding Oracle Vulnerabilities](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/merget) — Robert Merget, Juraj Somorovsky, Nimrod Aviram, Craig Young, Janis Fliegenschmidt, Jörg Schwenk, Yuval Shavitt — Reduces oracle probes and uses graph clustering to discover and classify TLS padding-oracle behaviour at Internet scale. **Score: 79.9/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [Postcards from the Post-HTTP World: Amplification of HTTPS Vulnerabilities in the Web Ecosystem](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8835223) — Stefano Calzavara, Riccardo Focardi, Matus Nemec, Alvise Rabitti, Marco Squarcina — Models how weak dependencies, subdomains and related origins amplify HTTPS compromise through transitive web trust. **Score: 79.2/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [The Betrayal at Cloud City: An Empirical Analysis of Cloud-Based Mobile Backends](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/alrawi) — Omar Alrawi, Chaoshun Zuo, Ruian Duan, Ranjita Pai Kasturi, Zhiqiang Lin, Brendan Saltaformaggio — SkyWalker derives cloud-backend inventories from apps, remotely vets their web attack surface and maps findings to responsible parties. **Score: 79.1/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [Provoking browser quirks with behavioural fuzzing](https://portswigger.net/research/provoking-browser-quirks-with-behavioural-fuzzing) — Gareth Heyes — Generates large Unicode and DOM test spaces to reveal browser parser differentials useful for XSS, sandbox and filter-bypass research. **Score: 74.6/100 — Tooling or methodology contribution.**
-   [Measuring and Analyzing Search Engine Poisoning of Linguistic Collisions](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2019.00025) — Matthew Joslin, Neng Li, Shuang Hao, Minhui Xue, Haojin Zhu — Uses misspellings that are valid words in another language to evade autocorrection and poison search results. **Score: 72.0/100 — Meaningful extension.**
